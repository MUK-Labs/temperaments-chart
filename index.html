How to read this atlasMethod, sources & listening models34 references

Evidence has a place. Preference needs a source.

A publication in Venice documents a text in Venice. It does not establish a uniform Venetian tuning. The timeline therefore shows dated evidence, with a movable viewing window, rather than fixed historical “reigns” of individual temperaments.

The map uses approximate modern city centers and a coastline without political boundaries. Rome has two source-location records at the same coordinates; they share one marker. Werckmeister's 1691 publication names Frankfurt and Leipzig and has no single map point in the source dataset.

Hear a model, keep its limits in view.

Listening links open the companion Temperament Explorer in a new tab, using its harpsichord sound. A link may present a numerical model supported by a text, a representative reconstruction, or a modern comparison. Its label tells you which.

Neither Bach's title The Well-Tempered Clavier nor the ability to play in every key determines a unique temperament. Equal division is not synonymous with “well-tempered.” Read the source note before attributing a listening model to a person.
